### Headline and protocol qualifications

A-MemGuard's abstract reports attack-success reductions above 95%, but Table 1 separates retrieval, thought, and task outcomes. For GPT-4o-mini with DPR on EHRAgent, retrieval attack success falls from 100% to 2.13%, while task-level success remains 36.17%. The annotation therefore does not generalize retrieval suppression to uniform end-to-end protection. MemoryArena §4.3 describes gains in web search and formal reasoning, while Table 3 shows backend- and metric-dependent results; the annotation retains that heterogeneity rather than treating every memory system as beneficial.

COLLABMEM's average-score finding is consistent with Table 5 within its fixed harness; it does not establish a universal product ranking. Its limitations explicitly question the compression metric. The inspected official record supports a year-only date, without an invented submission day. MemoryArena's proceedings and v2 HTML omit the title's MemoryArena prefix; the linked arXiv record retains it. They remain one work.

GameSmith uses a stronger teacher and engineered game templates; SPSD trains language models from separately trained search experts rather than putting the language model into expert self-play. ReSight separates exploration-set expansion from held-out reuse. ExpRAG's self-experience helps ALFWorld but hurts ScienceWorld. MLE-World's primary search tasks were seen in surrogate training, and its baseline lacks the same prior execution corpus. These qualifications limit the corresponding headline claims without treating untested generalization as a demonstrated failure.

### Interpretation and publication distinctions

Skill∞ revises a meta-skill, but external scores, budgets, and reviewer responsibilities remain fixed. MultiEvoSkill's WikiHow, ToolAlpaca, and WebShop adapters score workflow nodes; their F1 scores must not be relabeled interactive success. MiniWeb explicitly reports that skill-only fine-tuning fails to transfer well to full tasks; planner composition and filtered benchmark denominators matter.

Reasoning Cache's title refers to continued improvement within one problem using trained summary-conditioned inference, not ongoing deployment weight updates. Its v2 changed IMO-AnswerBench grading after earlier parser errors. The generalization-gap study reports both plateaus and a larger-model revision condition near oracle supervision. The robotics case study does not eliminate human intervention: people repaired the evaluator and surrounding machinery, and one target task never succeeded.

Memory Tax (arXiv 2610.34073) is distinct from MemoryTax (2610.29823), with different authors and experiments. Its main settings are long-history memory benchmarks, including non-conversational state changes; a stronger retrieval baseline does not establish that memory is generally unnecessary. The quadruped study finds no universally best curriculum and counts student environment steps; PAIRED's antagonist adds simulation work. REUSE derives simultaneous error control under explicit independence and protected-feedback assumptions. Its Covertype holdout estimates population improvement, and its adapted comparison schemes are not executions of the original SICA/DGM/PACE systems. Selected draft-informed cross-checks supported these qualifications.

The [official CLEA 2026 program](https://clea-neurips.github.io/) lists REM as an accepted poster and states a double-blind review process. Venue status has been updated accordingly, explicitly as a workshop acceptance rather than NeurIPS main-conference publication. No official implementation was independently verified.

The continual-learning study tests one shared harness across four short, ordered task streams: database-topic changes, conflicting BFCL workflows, different environments and their combination. Each group contributes fifty training tasks. Checkpoints are evaluated on all groups, including ones not yet encountered, with eight rollouts per held-out instance. Those repeated evaluations measure a frozen checkpoint’s behavior; they are not eight independent runs of the evolution process. The inspected paper does not report sensitivity across independently repeated learning runs or alternative block orders.

Its metrics also require care. Average advantage weights all groups and post-seed checkpoints equally, rather than measuring only the arriving tasks. Plasticity is block-end accuracy minus the seed, so it includes any earlier transfer. Forgetting compares block end with the final checkpoint and clips improvements to zero; it can miss an intermediate decline that later recovers. First-group interference and last-group forgetting are structurally zero. Plasticity and forgetting share the block-end score, so their negative correlation across twelve method/stream points is partly structurally coupled and does not establish a universal causal law.

GEPA has the largest mean gain and plasticity in the reported setup, while ReasoningBank has better mean stability. Meta-Harness’s larger editable surface is not consistently better; its apparent stability in some streams accompanies rejected updates and limited learning. Removing its validation gate worsens stability, but full matched-cost comparisons and exact optimizer configurations are not supplied. These are adapted methods under a shared actor and environment interface, not evidence that every implementation of the original systems has the same behavior.

Prompt histories and clarification traces illustrate plausible interference. SQLite-specific global rules appear before BFCL tasks arrive, and later direct-tool instructions displace earlier clarification behavior. Character shares track where text originated, not the semantic value of every retained line. There is no isolated rule-removal experiment attributing each accuracy change to a specific edit. GEPA also retains earlier rules successfully in one distant-domain stream, showing that overwriting is not inevitable in every run.

The top-one ReasoningBank variant always retrieves and has no abstention threshold, so foreign retrieval is inevitable before a new group has populated memory. Once distinct domains are populated, cross-domain retrieval and measured forgetting can vanish. Conflicting BFCL workflows remain intermixed, with 52%/46% opposite-workflow retrieval. Similarity means support a practical routing problem but do not prove that every possible threshold or richer feature fails; a threshold intervention is not evaluated. Interleaving is also mixed: it reduces GEPA’s aggregate gain but raises Meta-Harness’s aggregate terminal gain while still hurting one database group. These findings support bounded interference diagnoses, not a claim that all nonparametric adaptation is incapable of continual learning.

MemComposer trains the composer’s parameters while freezing the executor, retriever and bank. It selects and orders zero to ten memories, without rewriting them or updating the bank. Digests of roughly 1–1.4K tokens go to the composer, while selected full experiences of roughly 2.3–3K tokens go to the executor. Candidate retrieval uses the initial user opening, so this is not a policy that continually reselects memories as an interaction unfolds.

The bank is built from training trajectories. The reviewer receives reference answers, evaluator feedback and, for unsuccessful attempts, a successful sibling when available. These are externally grounded training signals, not wholly self-generated correctness labels. Structural checks validate review references and formatting, not every causal explanation. Own-task memories are excluded during composer training, but distinct benchmark tasks can share customers and orders. One appendix case reuses exact API arguments from the same customer/order. The split therefore does not uniformly test unseen-entity abstraction, although comparison methods share the same candidate pool.

ExPO uses eight sampled compositions and eight executor trials per composition. Groups with identical task reward are dropped even if their memory penalties differ. The maximum count penalty is below one success increment, but its ordering argument assumes equal format penalties. The reported setup has one training seed; four test trials per task are repeated evaluation of a learned policy, not four independent training runs. The paper’s pass-4 metric requires success on all four trials, rather than success on at least one.

Paired task-level bootstrap intervals preserve dependence between executors by resampling shared tasks within each domain. Pooled pass-1 contrasts favor the learned policy, but the strongest per-executor intervals include zero: approximately +5.9 points versus Memento for Qwen, with a −0.8 to 12.7 interval, and +4.6 versus MemRL for Ministral, with a −1.0 to 10.4 interval. Nonsignificance is not proof of equivalence. Some pass-4 comparisons also remain inconclusive. On ALFWorld, 82.0% versus 79.7% amounts to three additional successes among 133 games, without a reported interval for that contrast.

The heuristic retrieval count is selected using test-set macro success from a small candidate grid, giving those baselines favorable test-dependent selection. MemRL is adapted to a fixed bank with writeback disabled. Memento’s representation truncates inputs at 512 tokens, shorter than the composer’s digests, and its model capacity and training observations differ. These controls share the retrieval candidates but do not isolate every modeling or compute factor.

The 16.9% and 4.9% savings count executor memory tokens, not total inference or training cost. Composer input/output, retrieval, bank construction, training and API costs are outside that number. Domain success is macro-averaged, while memory tokens are averaged over the unequal task counts. Ordering replays hold the selected set fixed, but training credit still entangles selection with order. Cross-domain, executor and memory-bank results include tradeoffs and a negative retail contrast; stronger memory generators do not yield monotonic gains. The official README lists separate environments, checkpoints and data, with upstream licensing conditions; availability was inspected, not reproduced.

Harness² keeps its editor instructions, decision rules and weights fixed while accumulating an improvement library. The execution harness has eight editable components, and every new task starts from the common base. Parallel budget k creates k reference, k invariant, k domain and k composed executions: 4k executor sessions before counting improvement calls. Sequential refinement carries complete history and can restore earlier candidates. The final selector at k greater than one compares anonymized deliverables without their origin or official scores. A single candidate probe supplies behavioral evidence, not a reliable correctness oracle.

The main study uses three shuffled streams and four executor/host combinations. Its selected test sets contain 100 WorkBuddy tasks, 34 JobBench tasks and 100 LAB tasks. Online libraries accumulate within a stream without official local grading labels; the offline Meta-Harness baseline uses separate training labels. WorkBuddy’s judge shares one executor family, and JobBench’s judge is also the default editor family. LAB reports criterion-level judgments rather than executable success or real legal outcomes. These dependencies qualify interpretation without proving biased results.

Main aggregate scores generally improve, but Harness² is below Meta-Harness for Gemini under Codex on LAB. Per-session step counts do not include all executions and editor calls. Equal budget k gives best-of-k fewer executions, so comparisons at equal k are not equal-compute tests. The token-cost plot addresses measured totals for selected configurations, not universal efficiency. Removing composition also removes its execution, while deleting retained harness components without reoptimization measures their contribution to an already improved system; neither should be interpreted as a fully isolated, cost-matched intervention.

The leave-one-out analysis reruns each task after the stream with its own entry removed but with later-task entries available. Its gains are explicitly retrospective, not an estimate of causal online learning. Cross-model experiments transfer an adopted harness to another executor on the same tasks. Source-line overlap measures provenance, not the correctness or usefulness of copied experience. The appendix documents selected outputs substantially worse than available candidates, incorrect rules shared by every proposal, self-written checks that miss benchmark semantics, and effort spent on artifacts absent from the scoring criteria. The example plugin appends warnings rather than blocking execution.

The pinned [release experiment guide](https://github.com/google-research/harness2/blob/6868817518876de8fd170c6a8354daca273b6191/docs/EXPERIMENTS.md) reports a release-validation rollout that fetched its task’s public rubric. Network access is unrestricted by default; separate network controls are recommended. This is evidence about the released setup, not proof of contamination in historical paper experiments. The release also keeps failed evaluations unscored, while the paper’s failure example counts unparsed WorkBuddy verdicts as zero. Its LAB count, locally generated partitions, default editor and download-time JobBench revision differ from the reported experimental setup. Reproduction therefore requires explicit configuration and version reconciliation, not merely a successful installation.
